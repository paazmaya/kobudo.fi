Known sources are very sparse on _other_ named students of Yabiku Moden; most explicitly name only Taira. Here is what can be grounded.

## 1. Yabiku Moden as Taira’s kobudō source

- Japanese Ryūkyū kobudō history summaries agree that **Yabiku Moden (屋比久孟伝)** founded the **Ryūkyū Kobujutsu Kenkyūkai** in the Taishō era and was Taira’s main kobudō teacher. [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%90%89%E7%90%83%E5%8F%A4%E6%AD%A6%E8%A1%93)
- The Japanese Ryūkyū kobujutsu entry notes:
  - 「まず、屋比久孟伝が大正初期に琉球古武術研究会を設立し、その弟子の平信賢も1940年（昭和15年）に保存振興会を設立している。」 – “First, Yabiku Moden established the Ryukyu Kobujutsu Kenkyūkai in the early Taishō period, and **his disciple Taira Shinken** founded the Preservation Society in 1940.” [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%90%89%E7%90%83%E5%8F%A4%E6%AD%A6%E8%A1%93)
- A detailed English lineage article on Yabiku states that _among his students_ was **“the founder of Ryukyu Kobudo Hozon Shinko Kai, Taira Shinken”**, who “carried on the work started by his teacher” and added his own contributions. [ryukyukobudousa](https://www.ryukyukobudousa.com/lineage/yabiku-moden)

So for your genealogy:  
**Chinen Sanda (Yamani‑ryū bō) → Yabiku Moden → Taira Shinken → Inoue Motokatsu / others.** [kobudo-arakaki](https://kobudo-arakaki.com/about/)

## 2. Other students of Yabiku: what is explicitly attested?

Japanese and English sources that focus on Yabiku himself (rather than on Taira) are limited. The clearest points:

- Yabiku’s teachers are named (Itosu Ankō for karate; Chinen Sanda, Tawata/Tawada Shinboku, Kanagusuku Sanda for kobudō). [ryukyukobudousa](https://www.ryukyukobudousa.com/lineage/yabiku-moden)
- The Ryukyu‑kobudo USA lineage page calls **Taira Shinken “one of his most famous students”**, but does **not** list other named disciples. [ryukyukobudousa](https://www.ryukyukobudousa.com/lineage/yabiku-moden)
- The general Ryūkyū kobudō articles that mention Yabiku’s Kenkyūkai go directly from Yabiku to Taira when describing organizational lineage. [kobudo-arakaki](https://kobudo-arakaki.com/about/)

There are _likely_ other students (he taught karate and kobudō at Bito Elementary and in teacher‑training contexts), but those names are not clearly tied to later named styles in readily searchable Japanese or library‑oriented sources. Current evidence only allows you to state with confidence that:

- **Taira Shinken** is a documented direct kobudō student of Yabiku.
- Other later kobudō founders (Matayoshi Shinkō/Shinpō, Uehara Seikichi, etc.) draw on **the same Yamani and Chinen lines**, but are not explicitly described as Yabiku’s direct deshi in the sources above. [koryu-uchinadi](https://www.koryu-uchinadi.com/yamane-ryu/)

## 3. Styles that continue the “Yabiku stream”

Even though we can’t name many more Yabiku students, you can still map styles that **inherit his material indirectly**:

- **Taira line (Ryūkyū Kobujutsu / Ryukyu Kobudo Hozon Shinkōkai)** – Taira’s 42‑kata syllabus is explicitly said to derive from Yabiku’s Kenkyūkai corpus plus later gathering; this is the most direct institutional continuation of Yabiku’s kobujutsu work. [ryukyukobujutsuhozonshinkokai](https://www.ryukyukobujutsuhozonshinkokai.org/012_en_keifu.html)
- **Okinawan Ryūkyū Kobudō (Akamine / Nakamoto line)** – Akamine Eisuke studied Yamani‑line bō directly from Chinen’s students and then under Taira; Nakamoto’s Bunbukan tradition presents itself as preserving “Okinawan Traditional Kobudō” with heavy emphasis on Chinen–Yabiku–Taira material. [karatekaikan](https://karatekaikan.jp/products/book-okinawatradition-kobudo)
- **Yamane‑ryū (Yamanni‑ryū)** – Yamane‑ryū bōjutsu does not come from Yabiku but shares the same Chinen Sanda root; Yabiku was one of the earliest to _organize_ this material into a “Ryūkyū kobudō” framework, which later Taira and the Yamane revivalists both draw on. [mineralogicalrecord](https://mineralogicalrecord.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/wilson/pdfs/Yamanni.pdf)

You can therefore present the chart as:

- Chinen Sanda (Yamane‑ryū) → Yabiku Moden → Taira Shinken →
  - Ryūkyū Kobudō Hozon Shinkōkai / Inoue line,
  - Okinawan Ryūkyū Kobudō (Akamine, then Nakamoto),
  - plus influence on other kobudō founders who took kata from Taira.

But right now, beyond **Taira**, there are **no solid, sourced lists of other named students of Yabiku Moden and the styles they created** in Japanese library‑type references.
