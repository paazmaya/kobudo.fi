# Kata names

## How to read this mapping

- Same teacher name in the kata (周氏, 佐久川, 北谷屋良, 津堅, 添石, etc.) almost always indicates a shared historical root, even when choreography diverges. [okinawa.skr](https://okinawa.skr.jp/page_20240107183209?frame_id=20)
- Romanizations vary: 周氏の棍 = Shūshi / Shushi no kon; 添石の棍 = Soeishi / Sueyoshi / Shiishi no kon; 津堅棒・津堅砂掛けの棍 = Tsuken / Chikin / Tsuken Sunakake no kon. [jundokan-federation](https://jundokan-federation.com/doc/wukf_kobudo_kata_list-en.pdf)
- Post‑war schools often created “sho/dai/ni” variants from one older form, so multiple versions may still be the same kata family. [blackbeltwiki](https://blackbeltwiki.com/bo-staff-katas)

**Key lineages in the table**

- Taira line: 琉球古武道保存振興会系 (平信賢系統). [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%90%89%E7%90%83%E5%8F%A4%E6%AD%A6%E8%A1%93)
- Matayoshi: 又吉古武道／金硬流唐手沖縄古武術. [youtube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RKwym6QjRbg)
- Karate weapon curricula: e.g. Shōrin‑ryū, Isshin‑ryū, Shotokan groups that adopted Okinawan bō kata. [blackbeltwiki](https://blackbeltwiki.com/bo-staff-katas)

## Sai kata family relationships (mainly Taira line)

For sai, there is an unusually clear internal analysis of how Taira’s kata relate to each other, from a Ryukyu kobudō research group (武集館). They start from the eight sai kata attributed to Taira, note that one (慈元の釵) is his own creation, and then analyze similarities among the remaining “old” forms. Combined with lineage lists, you can map these relationships as follows. [bushukan.cloudfree](https://bushukan.cloudfree.jp/sai/)

From this analysis, a simple internal map emerges:

- **Hamahiga family**: 浜比嘉の釵 → 浜御殿屋可阿の釵, 端多小の釵; these share most of their structure and can be seen as elaborations or reorganizations of Hamahiga. [bushukan.cloudfree](https://bushukan.cloudfree.jp/sai/)
- **Tsuken–Chatan–Tawada family**: 津堅志多伯の釵, 北谷屋良の釵, 多和田の釵; another tight cluster with many common phrases. [bushukan.cloudfree](https://bushukan.cloudfree.jp/sai/)
- **Kochijo–Jigen pair**: 湖城の釵 ↔ 慈元の釵; Jigen is a Taira‑era creative adaptation of Kochijo to a special manji‑sai weapon. [bushukan.cloudfree](https://bushukan.cloudfree.jp/sai/)

These kata names (Hamahiga, Chatan Yara, Tsuken, Tawada, Kochijo) also appear in large multi‑organization kobudō kata lists, confirming that other schools have incorporated versions of the same families. [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%90%89%E7%90%83%E5%8F%A4%E6%AD%A6%E8%A1%93)
